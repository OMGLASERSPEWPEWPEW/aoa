## What They Do

Map-based art discovery app that helps users find nearby galleries, museums, artist-run spaces, exhibitions, and events. Positioned as "AllTrails for art" — a tool for curious people to explore a city's art scene without insider knowledge. Users can discover exhibitions, plan personalized art walks, and uncover hidden gems. The core promise is making the art world feel "less like a gated institution and more like an open invitation."

## Target Audience

- Curious, creative people who want to explore local art
- Art-curious non-experts who don't have gallery connections
- Gallery-hoppers and opening night regulars
- Weekend wanderers looking for guided cultural outings
- Travelers discovering art in a new city

**Overlap with AoA:** Very high on the discovery/map side. Artwrld is the closest analog to AoA's map-centric approach, just applied to visual art instead of theater. The "open invitation" positioning mirrors AoA's anti-gatekeeping philosophy.

## UX Analysis

### Onboarding
Low-friction, utility-first. Users see nearby art opportunities immediately — no long setup flow. Choose a city and start browsing. Minimal account dependency for initial discovery.

### Core Loop
**Open app → see nearby exhibition → save it → inspect on map → plan walk → go in person → discover more nearby → repeat.** Strong action-oriented loop — discovery leads directly to physical exploration. The "Exhibition of the Day" creates a daily hook.

### Map/Discovery UX
Map is the core organizing metaphor — the entire product revolves around geographic discovery. Uses map + list hybrid: browse spatially on the map or search by date/curation. Saved places create a personal map/itinerary layer.

**Key design decisions worth studying:**
- Map as the primary navigation (not a secondary view)
- Saved items visible on the map (not just in a list)
- Art walks as planned routes through multiple pins
- Editor's picks provide curated entry points for overwhelmed users

**Risks in this model:**
- Map browsing can become cluttered in dense areas
- Content density varies wildly by neighborhood
- Requires constant listing freshness to stay useful

### Social Features
Minimal social layer. "Connected discovery" positioning suggests future social features, but currently limited to sharing. No profiles, no follows, no community reviews.

### Mobile Experience
Mobile-first and designed for in-the-moment urban exploration. The promise of "hidden gems" and "art walks" is naturally mobile-native — supports wayfinding, ambient discovery, and spontaneous detours. The experience depends heavily on content freshness and local listing density.

## Strengths

- Clear product metaphor — "AllTrails for art" is instantly understandable
- Map-first UX solves a real discovery problem
- Strong anti-gatekeeping positioning resonates with non-expert audiences
- Action-oriented — browsing leads to physical visits
- City-by-city coverage makes the app feel locally relevant
- Art walks as planned routes convert discovery into experiences
- Founder has institutional art world credibility (Smithsonian background)
- 800+ galleries NYC, 300+ LA — meaningful listing density

## Weaknesses

- **Early stage** — only live in NYC and LA. No Chicago
- **Content dependence** — app is only as good as its listings freshness
- **No tracking/logging** — can't mark exhibitions as "visited" or rate them
- **No social features** — no reviews, no follows, no community
- **No AI/personalization** — no taste learning, no algorithmic recommendations
- **No progression** — no gamification, badges, or achievement system
- **Apple-only** (iOS) — excludes Android users
- **Curation burden** — manual content gathering doesn't scale easily
- **No business model yet** — sustainability uncertain
- **Premium conversion uncertain** — "studio visits" is a narrow premium hook

## What AoA Can Learn

### Steal This
- **"AllTrails for X" positioning** — This metaphor works brilliantly. AoA could position as "AllTrails for theater" or lean into the guided exploration angle
- **Exhibition of the Day** — A "Show of the Day" on AoA app open would create daily engagement without requiring user effort
- **Art walks as planned routes** — AoA could offer "theater walks" through Chicago neighborhoods (see 3 storefront theaters in Wicker Park in one evening)
- **Map as primary navigation** — Artwrld validates the map-first approach AoA already uses. This is the right bet
- **Anti-gatekeeping messaging** — "The art world should feel less like a gated institution and more like an open invitation" — AoA should echo this for theater
- **Editor's picks for overwhelmed users** — Curated entry points help new users who don't know where to start

### Avoid This
- **No tracking** — Artwrld can tell you what to see but can't remember what you've seen. AoA must have both sides
- **No social** — Discovery without community feels lonely. AoA's social layer is essential
- **Apple-only** — AoA's PWA approach is smarter for reach
- **Content-only moat** — Artwrld's value is entirely in its listings. AoA's AI mentor, progression system, and social features create a stickier product

### Differentiation Opportunity
- AoA applies Artwrld's map-first discovery model to **theater** — a domain Artwrld doesn't touch
- AoA adds **everything Artwrld lacks**: tracking, social, AI mentor, progression, content
- AoA's **Chicago focus** means deeper local density than Artwrld can achieve in any single city
- AoA's **AI mentor** provides personalized guidance that Artwrld's editor's picks can't match
- AoA's **belt progression** creates long-term engagement hooks Artwrld doesn't have
