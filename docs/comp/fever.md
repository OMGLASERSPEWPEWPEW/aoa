**Category:** art-culture-discovery / deal-ticketing
**URL:** https://feverup.com
**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Web
**Founded:** 2014
**Headquarters:** New York / Madrid
**Status:** Active — $527M+ funding, $1.8B valuation, 40+ countries
**Founders:** Ignacio Bachiller Ströhlein, Francisco Hein, Pep Gomez, Alexandre Perez Casares

---

## What They Do

Live-entertainment discovery and ticketing platform that helps users find and book curated local experiences. What makes Fever unique is they also **produce their own events** — including the hugely successful Candlelight concert series, immersive exhibitions, and other original formats. This makes them both a marketplace AND a content creator. They operate in 40+ countries with 150,000+ experiences and serve 125M+ users.

## Target Audience

- Urban experience-seekers looking for interesting things to do
- Date-night planners and "what should we do this weekend?" crowd
- Immersive experience enthusiasts (Candlelight, Van Gogh exhibitions, etc.)
- Not specifically theater-focused — broader cultural entertainment

**Overlap with AoA:** Medium. Fever's "curated local experiences" positioning overlaps with AoA's discovery mission. Their personalization and recommendation engine is relevant. But Fever is broad entertainment, not theater-specific, and has no tracking/social/progression features.

## UX Analysis

### Onboarding
Quick path to booking — browse, discover, buy. Location-based: shows nearby events immediately. Personalization builds over time as the app learns preferences. Low friction, purchase-oriented.

### Core Loop
**Open app → browse curated nearby events → get personalized suggestions → book ticket → attend → rate → get better suggestions → repeat.** Strong because each interaction improves personalization. Original events (Candlelight, immersive) create FOMO and urgency.

### Map/Discovery UX
Not map-centric — discovery is feed/category-based with location as a filter. Curated editorial approach: "city specialists and data scientists" select what to promote. More like an experience magazine than a map.

### Social Features
Minimal. No friend lists, no activity feeds, no reviews, no community. Purely individual discovery and booking.

### Mobile Experience
Mobile-first. Optimized for browse-to-book in minutes. Fast checkout, mobile tickets. Strong mobile UX for impulsive "what should we do tonight?" decisions.

## Strengths

- **Original content moat** — Candlelight concerts are a recognizable brand. Producing events gives Fever supply control and higher margins
- **Massive scale** — 150K+ experiences, 40+ countries, 125M+ users
- **Hybrid personalization** — combines AI with human curation (city specialists)
- **Mobile-first UX** — fast path from discovery to purchase
- **B2B revenue diversification** — not dependent on consumer ticketing alone
- **Well-funded** — $527M+ raised, $1.8B valuation
- **Cross-category breadth** — covers concerts, food, nightlife, immersive, theater, comedy

## Weaknesses

- **No tracking or logging** — doesn't remember what you've attended
- **No social features** — no friends, no reviews, no community
- **No progression/gamification** — flat engagement model
- **No educational content** — assumes you know what you want
- **Theater is a minor category** — Fever emphasizes immersive/pop-up over traditional theater
- **Support complaints** — refunds and cancellation friction are common issues
- **Experience quality varies** — proprietary events are polished; third-party quality is inconsistent
- **Not theater-specific** — too broad to serve theater newcomers specifically
- **"Exclusive" can disappoint** — sometimes "secret events" don't match expectations

## What AoA Can Learn

### Steal This
- **Hybrid curation model** — Fever combines algorithm with human expertise (city specialists). AoA's AI mentor + curated content is similar — validate this approach
- **Original content as moat** — AoA's belt progression curriculum and learning content are its "original content." This creates value competitors can't copy
- **FOMO mechanics** — Fever's "secret events" and limited-availability framing drives urgency. AoA could use "closing this weekend" or "only 3 performances left" urgency cues
- **Two-click path to action** — Whatever AoA's key action is (add to watchlist, start mentor chat), it should be two taps maximum from discovery
- **City specialist model** — For Chicago launch, having a "Chicago theater specialist" voice (the AI mentor) matches Fever's approach but with personality

### Avoid This
- **Breadth without depth** — Fever covers everything but masters nothing. AoA should master theater first
- **No community** — Fever is purely transactional. AoA must build social features
- **No memory** — Fever doesn't track your history. AoA must celebrate your journey
- **Support as afterthought** — Fever gets support complaints. AoA should make support a feature

### Differentiation Opportunity
- AoA is **deep where Fever is wide** — AoA masters Chicago theater while Fever covers everything superficially
- AoA has **tracking, social, and progression** — three pillars Fever entirely lacks
- AoA's **AI mentor has personality** — Fever's personalization is algorithmic and anonymous
- AoA's **educational content** develops taste — Fever assumes you already have it
- AoA could be the app that feeds INTO Fever/TodayTix — "I discovered it on AoA, bought tickets on Fever"
