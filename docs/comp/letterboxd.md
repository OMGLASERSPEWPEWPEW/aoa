# Letterboxd

**Category:** tracking-social
**URL:** https://letterboxd.com
**Platforms:** Web, iOS, Android, Apple TV
**Founded:** 2011
**Headquarters:** Auckland, New Zealand
**Status:** Active — majority owned by Tiny (60%) since 2023, founders retain 40%
**Founders:** Matthew Buchanan, Karl von Randow

---

## What They Do

Social film discovery and cataloging platform. Users log what they watch, rate and review films, curate lists, maintain a film diary, and follow other film lovers. Often described as "Goodreads for movies" but the real product is the combination of personal tracking, lightweight social networking, and discovery in one interface. This is the gold standard "Letterboxd for X" model that AoA is adapting for theater.

## Target Audience

- Film lovers and cinephiles who want a social layer on their viewing habits
- Casual viewers who want a watchlist and review tool
- Power users who want stats, lists, and community interaction
- Digitally engaged, film-interested users (skews younger, culturally aware)

**Overlap with AoA:** High — Letterboxd is AoA's stated UX model. Same core mechanic (track + rate + review + social) applied to a different art form. Letterboxd proves this model works at scale (30M+ users).

## UX Analysis

### Onboarding
Minimal friction — core features free, browse immediately. The product is self-explanatory: log, rate, review, follow. "Free forever" messaging lowers barriers. New users can start logging immediately without understanding the full feature set.

### Core Loop
**Watch film → log it → rate/review → see friends' reactions → discover next film → repeat.** Each watched film creates a new post, data point, and social object. The loop is self-reinforcing: the more you log, the better your stats and the more interesting your profile becomes to others. Discovery happens through activity streams, lists, reviews, and trending films.

### Map/Discovery UX
No map. Discovery is social-first: what are people I follow watching? What lists are trending? What's new this week? Theater showtimes were added but are secondary to the social discovery mechanism.

### Social Features
This is Letterboxd's core strength:
- **Following** — see friends' activity in a dedicated feed
- **Reviews** — can be commented on and liked
- **Lists** — publicly shareable, highly viral (Letterboxd lists regularly go viral on social media)
- **Profile as identity** — favorites, ratings, and review history create a "film taste" identity
- **Lightweight interaction** — social without being a full social network. No DMs, no stories, no algorithmic feed. Just film talk

### Mobile Experience
Web is the core experience. Mobile apps extend logging and browsing. Apple TV support for viewing-adjacent use. The mobile app is functional but web-first in design philosophy — larger screens show more context.

## Business Model

| Revenue Stream | Details |
|---------------|---------|
| Pro subscriptions | $19/year — ad-free, advanced stats, streaming filters |
| Patron subscriptions | $49/year — Pro benefits + additional perks |
| Video Store | Transactional film rentals (launched Dec 2025) |

**Pricing:** Free forever for core features. Pro $19/year. Patron $49/year. The free tier is deliberately broad — most users never need to pay. Monetization targets power users who want analytics and ad-free experience.

## Strengths

- **Clear product-market fit** — solves a specific, underserved need brilliantly
- **Network effects** — each review, list, and follow makes the platform more valuable
- **Identity utility** — profiles become a form of cultural self-expression
- **Low friction, high utility** — free tier is genuinely useful, not crippled
- **Cultural momentum** — grew from 3M (2020) to 30M+ (2026) users
- **Lists go viral** — organic distribution through social media
- **Clean, opinionated design** — distinctive visual identity
- **Discovery + record-keeping** in one product — rare combination

## Weaknesses

- **Film-only** — no live performances, no venues, no locations
- **Web-first design** on a mobile-first world — mobile app feels secondary
- **Ads on free tier** create pressure to upgrade
- **Discovery depends on network quality** — if you follow boring people, discovery is boring
- **No AI/personalization** — recommendations are social, not algorithmic
- **No map/location features** — purely digital, no physical world connection
- **UI shows its age** — design hasn't evolved dramatically since 2013 era
- **No progression/gamification** — no badges, levels, or achievement system

## What AoA Can Learn

### Steal This
- **The diary/log as identity** — Letterboxd proves that tracking what you experience becomes a form of self-expression. AoA's show log should feel like building an identity, not just checking boxes
- **Lists as viral content** — User-created lists are Letterboxd's growth engine. AoA should plan for "Best storefront shows 2026" lists that get shared on social media
- **Free tier generosity** — Don't cripple the free experience. Let core tracking/social be free, monetize analytics and premium features
- **Half-star ratings** — 0.5-5.0 scale gives more expressive range than 1-5 integers
- **Activity feed design** — Lightweight social feed (not algorithmic, not noisy) is the right model for a niche community
- **Profile as showcase** — Favorites, stats, and history on your profile creates investment
- **"Always free" positioning** — This messaging builds trust and lowers adoption barriers

### Avoid This
- **Web-first in a mobile world** — AoA should be mobile-first from day one. Theater discovery happens on phones
- **No location awareness** — Letterboxd doesn't know where you are. AoA's map is a massive advantage
- **No progression** — Letterboxd has no gamification. AoA's belt system fills a real gap
- **No AI** — Letterboxd relies on social discovery only. AoA's mentor is a differentiator

### Differentiation Opportunity
- AoA is **"Letterboxd for theater" with everything Letterboxd doesn't have**: map, AI mentor, progression, location awareness, and newcomer onboarding
- The live performance aspect creates unique features: venue profiles, distance, "what's playing tonight," local scene knowledge
- Belt progression adds a gamification layer Letterboxd deliberately chose not to build
- AI mentor provides personalized recommendations that Letterboxd's social-only approach can't match
