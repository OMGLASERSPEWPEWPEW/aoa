## What They Do

The official show discovery platform for Chicago-area theater, run by the League of Chicago Theatres. ChicagoPlays aggregates productions from 200+ member theaters into one searchable directory with performance dates, ticket links, venue pages, and an accessibility calendar. It is the canonical "what's playing in Chicago" resource — a nonprofit-run, member-submitted directory rather than a commercial marketplace.

## Target Audience

- Chicago theatergoers looking for what's on now or coming soon
- Visitors planning a theater outing in Chicago
- Accessibility-conscious patrons (dedicated access calendar)
- Theater-savvy users who already know they want to see a show

**Overlap with AoA:** Medium-high. ChicagoPlays is the official data source for Chicago theater listings. AoA needs this data but presents it differently — with AI curation, map discovery, social tracking, and newcomer guidance rather than a flat directory.

## UX Analysis

### Onboarding
None — it's a public directory. Visit the site, search for shows. No account needed. Very low friction but also no personalization.

### Core Loop
**Visit site → enter dates → scan results → filter by neighborhood/genre → click through to ticket purchase (on another site).** The loop is transaction-oriented: find show → buy ticket elsewhere. No retention mechanism, no tracking, no return incentive beyond needing to find another show.

### Map/Discovery UX
No map. Discovery is structured search — enter dates, browse results. Neighborhood filters provide geographic context but not visual/spatial discovery. Cards with large imagery improve scannability but can feel visually busy with many productions live simultaneously.

### Social Features
None. No accounts, no profiles, no reviews, no social anything.

### Mobile Experience
Described as "mobile-first" by the developer (Venture Industries) with custom filtering and event tools optimized for speed and imagery. Responsive design with card-based layouts. Still feels like a directory rather than an app experience.

## Strengths

- **Authority** — the official League platform. Canonical source for Chicago theater
- **Breadth** — 200+ companies, 1,000+ productions indexed
- **Accessibility calendar** — standout feature no competitor matches
- **Local specificity** — neighborhood filters match how Chicagoans think about going out
- **Member self-service** — theaters keep their own listings current
- **Adjacent to HotTix** — part of a broader League ecosystem for audience development

## Weaknesses

- **Just a directory** — no curation, no recommendations, no personalization
- **No map** — neighborhoods are text filters, not visual discovery
- **No social features** — no reviews, no tracking, no community
- **No accounts** — can't save shows, build a history, or get personalized results
- **Purchase happens elsewhere** — click-through to individual theater ticketing adds friction
- **No mobile app** — responsive web only
- **Data quality varies** — member-submitted content means inconsistent detail/imagery per theater
- **Coverage gaps** — non-League-member theaters and performances may be missing
- **No educational content** — assumes you already understand theater
- **Utilitarian UX** — date-first workflow serves decision-makers, not explorers

## What AoA Can Learn

### Steal This
- **Accessibility calendar** — AoA should surface accessibility information (ASL, audio-described, sensory-friendly performances). This is both a moral good and a competitive differentiator
- **Neighborhood as primary filter** — Chicagoans think in neighborhoods. AoA's map naturally supports this but should also have explicit neighborhood labels and filtering
- **Campaign moments** — "Year of Chicago Theatre" and "Theatre Thursdays" show the power of editorial campaigns. AoA's mentor could run themed recommendation campaigns
- **Data relationship** — ChicagoPlays is the canonical listing source. AoA might eventually partner with or pull from League data

### Avoid This
- **Directory without soul** — ChicagoPlays is useful but uninspiring. AoA must add personality, curation, and delight
- **No retention mechanism** — Users leave after finding a show and don't come back. AoA's tracking, social, and progression create reasons to return
- **No personalization** — Same results for everyone. AoA's AI mentor is the antidote

### Differentiation Opportunity
- AoA takes ChicagoPlays' listing breadth and adds **everything it lacks**: map discovery, AI curation, social tracking, progression, education, and personality
- ChicagoPlays is a **reference tool**; AoA is a **companion**
- AoA could position as "ChicagoPlays meets Letterboxd meets your theater-savvy friend"
- Potential **data partnership** with the League could give AoA authoritative listings
