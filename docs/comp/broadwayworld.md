## What They Do

The largest theater news, database, and community platform on the web. BroadwayWorld combines editorial journalism (news, interviews, reviews, features), a production database (show pages, grosses, cast info), and an active community forum system. It covers Broadway, Off-Broadway, touring productions, and regional theater across many US cities. Recently added a "My Shows" tracking feature for personal show logging.

## Target Audience

- Theater enthusiasts following Broadway news, reviews, and industry gossip
- Regional theater fans tracking local productions
- Industry professionals monitoring box office, casting, and production news
- Forum community members who discuss productions, awards, and insider topics

**Overlap with AoA:** Medium. BroadwayWorld serves engaged theater fans, not newcomers. Its regional coverage model (city-specific pages) is relevant to AoA's Chicago focus. The "My Shows" feature directly competes with AoA's tracking.

## UX Analysis

### Onboarding
No formal onboarding — it's a media site. Browse news, click articles, explore show pages. My Shows tracking presumably requires account creation but details are limited in public materials.

### Core Loop
**Check daily news → read reviews → discuss in forums → track shows via My Shows → follow box office → repeat.** The loop is news-driven: theater events (openings, closings, casting announcements) create daily content that drives return visits. Forums create community stickiness.

### Map/Discovery UX
No map. Discovery is editorial (what's BroadwayWorld writing about?) and database-driven (search shows, browse by season). Regional pages organize content by city but not spatially.

### Social Features
Forums are the primary social layer — active, passionate, sometimes contentious. My Shows adds personal tracking. Community moderation quality varies. No friend/follow system in the Letterboxd sense.

### Mobile Experience
Web-first, responsive design. No dedicated mobile app in evidence. The information-dense layout can feel overwhelming on mobile. Better suited to desktop browsing.

## Strengths

- **Largest theater media footprint** — dominant in news, reviews, and community
- **Deep database** — comprehensive show, grosses, and cast information
- **Active community** — forums create engagement and insider discussion
- **Regional coverage** — city-specific pages serve markets beyond NYC
- **Data depth** — box office grosses and analytics serve industry professionals
- **Always-on news cycle** — daily content drives repeat visits

## Weaknesses

- **Information overload** — dense, cluttered interface. Hard for casual users to navigate
- **Not beginner-friendly** — assumes deep theater knowledge and interest
- **No map** — no location-based discovery
- **No AI/personalization** — same content for everyone
- **Ad-heavy** — advertising can overwhelm the reading experience
- **Forum moderation challenges** — passionate communities can be unwelcoming
- **Web-only** — no mobile app
- **My Shows tracking is new/underdeveloped** — limited documentation on features
- **Editorial quality varies by region** — NYC coverage is deep; regional can be thin

## What AoA Can Learn

### Steal This
- **Regional coverage model** — BroadwayWorld's city-specific pages show how to organize theater content by location. AoA should study their Chicago page
- **Show database structure** — Cast, creative, dates, venue, reviews all on one page. AoA's show profiles should be similarly comprehensive
- **Community engagement** — Forums prove theater fans want to discuss, not just browse. AoA's social features should enable conversation
- **My Shows as validation** — BroadwayWorld adding personal tracking proves the market demand for "Letterboxd for theater"

### Avoid This
- **Information overload** — AoA must be clean, focused, and accessible. Not a news portal
- **Ad-supported model** — Creates bad UX and misaligned incentives. AoA should pursue freemium
- **No newcomer pathway** — BroadwayWorld serves insiders. AoA must serve newcomers first
- **Forum toxicity risk** — Unmoderated passionate communities can become hostile. AoA needs moderation from day one

### Differentiation Opportunity
- AoA is **not a news site** — it's a personal theater companion. Different product entirely
- AoA serves **newcomers** where BroadwayWorld serves insiders
- AoA's **map** provides spatial discovery BroadwayWorld doesn't have
- AoA's **AI mentor** provides personalized guidance BroadwayWorld's editorial can't match
- AoA's **belt progression** creates structured engagement BroadwayWorld doesn't attempt
