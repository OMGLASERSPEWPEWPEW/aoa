# Domain Research: The Art of Art

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Domain:** Theater discovery and education, starting with Chicago
**Source:** Conversation with founder (docs/convo-one.md) + web research

---

## 1. The Problem

People who are curious about theater — especially those taking their first acting classes — don't know how to enter the scene as audience members. The barrier isn't financial or logistical; it's **informational and social**. They don't know:

- What to see (which theaters, which plays, which playwrights matter)
- How to navigate the ecosystem (storefront vs. institutional, Equity vs. non-union)
- What the social norms are (can I talk to actors after? What's opening night like?)
- How theater fits into their life stage (the 20s hustle vs. 30s reckoning vs. 40s mastery)
- What's happening right now in their city

The result: people who would love theater never become regular theatergoers because the scene feels intimidating without a knowledgeable friend to guide them.

## 2. Users

### Primary Persona: The Curious Newcomer
- Taking acting classes (or just finished their first)
- 20s-40s, urban, curious about arts and culture
- Has seen a few shows but doesn't consider themselves "a theater person"
- Wants to be guided, not lectured
- Needs someone to say "go see THIS, at THIS theater, because YOU would love it"

### Secondary Personas
- **The Lapsed Theatergoer**: Used to go, fell off, wants to reconnect
- **The Relocator**: Moved to a new city, wants to find the local scene
- **The Theater Student**: In a program, wants to see everything, needs to understand the landscape professionally

### Anti-Personas
- Professional theater critics (they already know everything)
- Casual tourists looking for one Broadway-style show (too transactional)
- People looking for ticket discounts only (not the core value prop)

## 3. The Core Insight

Theater is the most accessible art form — you literally buy a ticket and walk in. But it **feels** inaccessible because:

1. **No curation layer exists for newcomers.** Existing platforms (Theater.Guide, ChicagoPlays.com, Chicago.Theater) are listings, not guides. They tell you what's playing, not what YOU should see.
2. **The storefront scene is invisible to outsiders.** Chicago's 200+ storefront theaters are where the magic happens, but they don't have marketing budgets. Discovery happens through word of mouth.
3. **The social layer is missing.** There's no Letterboxd/Goodreads for theater. You can't track what you've seen, rate it, see what friends recommend, or build a "want to see" list.
4. **Progression is undefined.** Unlike martial arts (belt colors) or gaming (levels), theater has no visible progression path for audience members. You go from "never been" to... nothing. No milestones, no unlocks, no sense of growth.

## 4. Domain Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Storefront theater** | Small company with 50-99 seat venue, Chicago's signature ecosystem |
| **Equity** | Actors' Equity Association, the union. "Equity card" = union membership |
| **Non-union / non-Eq** | Shows without union contracts, often storefront |
| **Opening night** | First public performance, often with reception after |
| **Dark night** | Night the theater is closed (usually Monday) |
| **Usher** | Volunteer who seats audience; many theaters offer free admission to ushers |
| **House** | The audience seating area, or the theater itself |
| **Call** | Audition notice ("open call" = anyone can audition) |
| **Callback** | Second audition after initial call |
| **Black box** | Flexible theater space with no fixed stage/seating |
| **Thrust** | Stage that extends into the audience on three sides |
| **Proscenium** | Traditional stage with audience facing one direction |
| **Devised** | Work created collaboratively by the ensemble, not from a script |

## 5. Emotional Arc

The user's journey maps to an emotional progression:

```
Intimidation → Curiosity → First Show → "I liked that" → Second Show →
"I have opinions" → Regular Attendance → "I know what I like" →
Community Connection → "I'm part of this" → Advocacy → "Come with me"
```

The app needs to meet users at each emotional stage and gently push them to the next.

## 6. Geographic Factors (Chicago)

### Why Chicago is the Perfect Launch City

- **200+ theater companies** — more than any US city except New York
- **Storefront theater ecosystem** — unique to Chicago, invisible to outsiders
- **Accessible pricing** — $15-40 for most storefront shows vs. $100+ on Broadway
- **Actor accessibility** — performers are in the lobby after shows, approachable
- **Geographic density** — many theaters clustered in neighborhoods (Lakeview, Andersonville, Wicker Park, Loop)
- **Strong acting class ecosystem** — Second City, iO, Acting Studio Chicago, Actors Centre, etc.

### Key Theater Neighborhoods

| Neighborhood | Character |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Loop/Downtown** | Major institutions (Goodman, Chicago Shakespeare, Broadway in Chicago) |
| **Lincoln Park** | Steppenwolf, Victory Gardens |
| **Lakeview/Boystown** | Smaller storefront companies |
| **Andersonville** | Neo-Futurists, Raven Theatre |
| **Wicker Park/Bucktown** | Experimental, younger companies |
| **Hyde Park** | Court Theatre (U of C affiliated) |
| **Evanston/Glencoe** | Writers Theatre, Northlight |

## 7. Life Stage Dynamics

From founder research (docs/convo-one.md):

| Stage | Theater Relationship | App Relevance |
|-------|---------------------|---------------|
| **20s** | Everything is new, saying yes to everything, friend groups form around theater, money is tight | Budget-conscious recs, social features, "try everything" progression |
| **30s** | Craft deepens, friend group thins, the commitment question, teaching as income | Curated recs, deeper content, career-adjacent features |
| **40s** | Elder status, mentoring, choosing carefully, legacy | Selective recommendations, community leadership features |

The app should ask for age during onboarding and tailor recommendations accordingly — not just "what shows" but "why this show matters at your stage."
